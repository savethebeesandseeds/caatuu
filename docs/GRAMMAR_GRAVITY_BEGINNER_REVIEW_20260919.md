# Grammar Gravity beginner-content review

Date: 19 September 2026. Scope: every current Grammar Gravity phrase in Czech,
Spanish, English from Spanish and Norwegian Bokmål. Mandarin does not declare
Grammar Gravity and has no corresponding agreement catalog to revise.

## Finding

The content was not consistently suitable for a new learner. Familiar words
were sometimes embedded in past-tense sentences, fronted location phrases,
relative clauses, idioms or additional case/word-order demands while retaining
a low complexity score. A short phrase could also contain specialized vocabulary
and be graded like a basic household word. Grammatical correctness and
beginner suitability are separate questions.

For example, Norwegian `Vi fant en stor stein på stranden.` had complexity 22,
the same as `en stor hund`. Its revised score is 52, while the short phrase is
10. English `Inside the box, there is a compass.` moved from 36 to 60, and
`On the next page, there are three riddles.` from 31 to 70. Czech
`zajímavá otázka k příběhu` moved from 27 to 54; Spanish `un dedal pequeño`
moved from 22 to 54 because the less familiar noun increases the task demand.

Scores remain editorial estimates on the existing 1–100 scale. They are not
CEFR certifications, measured recall probabilities or a claim that a particular
number of answers proves learning. The three badges remain intact.

## Editorial changes

All 1,104 existing examples were reviewed. The first editorial pass produced
1,242 examples, including 138 new short phrases and simple clauses. Existing IDs,
badges and richer contexts remain available; changed examples, families and
all four catalog revisions were updated. Native-speaker review and licensing
metadata were preserved.

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Czech | 249 | 18 | 267 | Everyday two-word agreement phrases, balanced across three genders |
| Spanish | 353 | 24 | 377 | Regular article and noun phrases, such as `el gato` and `las casas` |
| English from Spanish | 178 | 44 | 222 | 24 bare `There is/are` clauses, plus 12 simple `is/are` and eight `has/have` clauses at their existing badges |
| Norwegian Bokmål | 324 | 52 | 376 | 36 bare phrases and 16 short present-tense clauses; irregular `liten` and abstract advice vocabulary come later |

Czech and Spanish already had substantial short-phrase content. Their review
therefore concentrated on uncommon vocabulary, irregular agreement, possessives,
quantifiers and added context. Czech adds examples such as `malá kočka`,
`velký pes` and `nové tričko`. Four existing English glosses were clarified
across the Czech and Spanish banks, including the countable English rendering
`a pretty piece of embroidery`.

English now has a separate beginner existential family. This permits natural
sentence-initial `There is/are` without forcing the lowercase form from the
older fronted-location sentences into the same authored slot. Existing richer
examples remain at higher complexity. Spanish learner-base translations stay
separate from the English target and audit text.

Norwegian now distinguishes bare agreement, a short present-tense clause, and
a richer context. Examples include `en stor hund`, `et rødt eple`,
`nye bøker`, `Jeg har en ny bok.` and `Jeg ser store hus.` All 324 preexisting
examples were regraded individually for full task demand. The valid common/neuter
Bokmål convention is retained. An independent second AI review checked all 52
new examples and the four earlier bare starters without finding a definite
agreement or translation error.

After that first pass, the initial badge-one complexity windows contained
87 Czech, 43 Spanish, 41 English and 30 Norwegian examples. The opening
Norwegian window no longer contained the long narrative sentences that prompted
this work, but the opening and following pools still needed more breadth.

banks to **2,239**. All 1,242 examples from the first pass remain unchanged,
including their IDs, wording, slots, translations, badges and grades. Existing
material was not lowered in complexity to make the starting pools look larger.
All four catalogs are now revision 4; changed family revisions were incremented,
and new examples and families begin at revision 1.

The initial pool is the actual fresh badge-one window: minimum complexity plus

The following pool is the next range through complexity 45: 29–45 or 31–45

planning guides, not test pins, measured learning thresholds or permanent

## Runtime and grading corrections

The game already propagated each complete example's complexity into sequence,
meaning and form practice. The problem was chiefly the authored grading and
the shortage of short Norwegian/English contexts, with two additional runtime
defects discovered during review:

- Meaning distractors previously came from every badge-eligible example,
  including material outside the selected practice cohort. They now come from
  the selected cohort. A cohort with one meaning can borrow one distinct meaning
  at the same or lower badge/complexity. It does not borrow a much harder word
  merely to fill six buttons. The existing requirement for at least two valid
  meanings remains; all current catalogs provide suitable choices.
- Direct entry into meaning or form practice built the first queue using
  sequence-mode history. Startup now selects the requested mode before reading
  its history. Switching modes and changing answer counts preserve the scoped
  choices and separate learning evidence.

The optional editorial grading tool now recognizes finite clauses, past tense
and additional settings around agreement slots. These are proposal heuristics,
not a substitute for reading the material. Normal migration preserves authored
scores; only an explicit regrade recalculates them. No bulk regrade was run on
other games during this review.

History was not reset. Material already encountered can still appear as due
review after its editorial score increases. The revised starting windows govern
fresh introductions; they do not erase a returning learner's recorded work.

## Review and validation boundary

This is an AI-assisted editorial review, not native-speaker certification.
Every one of the 997 expansion examples also received an independent second
AI language review. This produced one final English naturalness correction,
`The school is nearby.`, before structural validation was repeated. Native
review requirements remain unchanged.

Primary references checked for relevant grammatical distinctions include:

- Czech: [ÚJČ adjective nový](https://m.prirucka.ujc.cas.cz/?ascii=0&dotaz=1&id=nov%C3%BD),
  [dva/dvě](https://prirucka.ujc.cas.cz/?id=670), and
  [můj variants](https://ssjc.ujc.cas.cz/search.php?heslo=m%C5%AFj&hsubstr=no).
- Spanish: [RAE article classes and paradigm](https://www.rae.es/gram%C3%A1tica-b%C3%A1sica/el-art%C3%ADculo/caracterizaci%C3%B3n/clases-y-paradigma),
  [grande](https://www.rae.es/dpd/grande) and
  [INTEF possessive determiners](https://descargas.intef.es/recursos_educativos/It_didac/Leng_Pri/5/10/03_Demostrativos_posesivos/los_determinantes_posesivos.html).
- English: British Council material on [existential there](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/it-there-dummy-subjects),
  [be](https://learnenglishteens.britishcouncil.org/grammar/a1-a2-grammar/be-present-past),
  and [quantifiers](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/a1-a2-grammar/quantifiers-few-a-few-little-a-bit),
  plus [Cambridge's embroidery entry](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/embroidery).
- Norwegian: NTNU's [adjective patterns](https://www.ntnu.edu/now/4/grammar),
  [past tense and marked adjectives](https://www.ntnu.edu/now/5/grammar),
  [definiteness and clause order](https://www.ntnu.edu/now/6/grammar),
  and [possessives](https://www.ntnu.edu/now/7/grammar),
  with [Bokmålsordboka's råd entry](https://ordbokene.no/nob/bm/r%C3%A5d).

These references support grammar and usage, not the numerical scores. Structural
checks validate complete slots, stable identities, grade propagation and
solvable choices; they cannot certify language quality. Regression tests use
synthetic grammar tasks and history to check behavior, without pinning current
phrases or live catalog counts. The Czech historical compatibility test now
protects stable identities and playable rounds rather than freezing old wording.

All four expanded banks passed full schema/round validation and 1,440 simulated
selections spanning all badges and both fresh and existing history. The focused
grammar runtime, curriculum, view, journey, progression integration and modern
course suite passed all 69 tests. All five course packs also passed the course
contract validator. The earlier focused grading tests cover the new context
heuristics, and the runtime tests protect choice scoping and mode history.

Changes remain local. No application build, publication, commit or generated
asset refresh was performed for this review. Offline integrity metadata must be
refreshed from the final combined source state when release work resumes.
